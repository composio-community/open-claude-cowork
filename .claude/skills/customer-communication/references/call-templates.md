# Call and Voicemail Templates

Wording for greetings and voicemails, plus the phone-technique reminders that shape a script.
Hours: **8am–5pm EST, Monday–Friday** (settled).

Brackets such as [Name] or [Date] below are fill-in slots in these templates. Replace every one with real data
(ask the user if missing); no bracket may remain in the final text.

## 1. Greetings

**Incoming call:** "Thank you for calling Morena Mia Beauty Group; this is [Name]. How can I assist you?"

**Outbound follow-up call:** "Hello, this is the Morena Mia Beauty Group customer service department. We are
trying to reach [Customer Name] about a purchase on [Date] at [Store]. We would like to hear about your
experience and answer any questions. You can call us back at 888-249-4189, 8am–5pm EST, Monday–Friday."

## 2. Voicemail

Keep to 7 short lines at most. Base form:

> Hello, this is Morena Mia Beauty Group Customer Service. We are trying to reach [Customer Name] about a
> recent purchase at [City]. Please call us back at 888-249-4189, 8am–5pm EST, Monday–Friday. Thank you.

For a case that already has a reference, use the structure in `SKILL.md` (who, the one fact, the next step,
how to reach us, callback number once more, a reassuring clause, thanks). When the voicemail confirms a
shipping address for a replacement, ask them to reply or call back with it.

## 3. Structure of a call

1. **Opening:** greeting, identification, purpose, check they have a minute.
2. **Gathering:** open questions, active listening, notes.
3. **Proposing:** offer options, explain the benefit, handle objections.
4. **Closing:** summarise what was agreed, confirm the actions, thank them, say goodbye.

## 4. Handling complaints on the phone

- Do not take it personally; let the customer finish.
- Repeat back what you understood so expectations line up.
- Offer concrete solutions with clear timelines.
- Clarify → agree on the amount or action → ask for commitment → handle objections (C.A.S.H.).
- Avoid trigger words: "policy", "no", "but". Lead with what you can do.
- Apologise near the end, after taking responsibility and offering the fix; then follow up.
- If emotions run high, let them vent, acknowledge the feeling, then move to problem-solving; if needed, pause and consult a superior.

## 5. Voice and tone

Smile while speaking, vary pace and volume, pronounce clearly, use pauses for emphasis, use silence to let
the customer think.
