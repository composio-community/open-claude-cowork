# Follow-up, Deadlines and Closing

What to say to the customer about contact cadence, deadlines and closing a case. Do not name internal
statuses or systems in customer channels (see `internal-ops.md`).

## 1. Cadence and Automatic Next-Contact Calculation (Monday–Friday Rule)

- **Business days only (`Monday–Friday`):** Customer Service operates strictly **Monday through Friday, 8am–5pm EST**. Weekends (Saturday and Sunday) are non-working days. **Never** schedule or promise a follow-up contact on a Saturday, a Sunday, or on the same day as today.
- **Resolution delivered** (product shipped or refund issued): send the deadline message and stop contacting
  the customer until the close date (if the close date falls on a Saturday or Sunday, roll it to the following **Monday**).
- **No resolution yet / waiting on the customer:** contact every business day, **Monday–Friday**, until the close date:
  - If today is **Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, or Thursday** $\to$ next contact is **tomorrow** (+1 day).
  - If today is **Friday** (or Saturday/Sunday) $\to$ next contact is the **following Monday** (skipping Saturday and Sunday).
  - If a ClickUp comment or tracking update indicates that a package or check will be delivered on date $X$, resume follow-ups on the **first business day (`Monday–Friday`) after $X$**.
- **The customer's bank gave them an exact number of days ($X$)** for a dispute or transfer: no daily contact.
  Schedule a single follow-up in $X$ days (if that date lands on a weekend, move it to the **following Monday**) and tell the customer that date.

In the message, say only when they will next hear from us (PENDING #4: do not name the channel mix).

## 2. Deadline message

- **Trigger:** 4 or more calendar days without a customer response.
- Send it **once**. If a deadline was already sent (visible in the history), do not send another; the case
  proceeds to closing.
- Content: keep it warm and factual. Say we have not been able to reach them about [matter], then frame what
  is still needed as ready-and-waiting, never as blame: *"Everything is ready on our side. Once we receive
  [X], we'll proceed right away."* State that we will close the case on [date] and that contacting us reopens
  it. Do not use "nothing is moving" or "we are waiting on you" phrasing.

## 3. Closing

- A case closes only when the customer **explicitly confirms**, by email or phone, that they received the
  product, the instructions or the refund. A carrier signature alone is not enough.
- No response after the deadline: the case closes without a resolution from the customer; if they
  reconnect, it is reopened. The message can say exactly that.
- Never promise a case is closed before the customer has confirmed.
- Even at closing, the farewell keeps the door open: never "fully closed", "nothing further you need to do",
  "no further action is required" or "this concludes your case". Close with an invitation, e.g. *"If anything
  else comes up, simply reply — we're always glad to help."* (SKILL.md §0.12).

## 4. Message pattern for a follow-up

1. Open with a warm one-sentence summary of where things stand (thank them first — don't re-open the mistake).
2. Say what is done and what is pending — frame the pending part as ready-and-waiting, never as blame
   ("nothing is moving", "we are waiting on you").
3. One next step with an owner and a date.
4. Say whether we will contact them again, and when.
